# Story Phases — The Carval Dossier

---

## Phase 1 — Prologue: Cassie Carval
Cassie Carval was once the Undersecretary of the Department of Public Works and Highways (DPWH). Years inside the system gave her access to documents few ever saw—budget reallocations, padded contracts, and flood control projects whose costs ballooned while communities remained submerged. Before resigning, Cassie compiled a comprehensive dossier detailing these anomalies, implicating not just DPWH officials but also members of Congress, the Senate, and other government offices. She believed that transparency required courage—and preparation.

---

## Phase 2 — The Invitation
On December 19, 2025, Cassie invited five people she once trusted to her mountain house in Benguet. Each held power, each had something to lose if the dossier became public. Cassie told them she possessed a hard copy of the files, secured in a safe inside her study. She framed the meeting as a final conversation—an opportunity for honesty before she decided her next move.

---

## Phase 3 — The Night Before (Part 1)
The evening unfolded over dinner and drinks. Cassie openly discussed the corruption she uncovered, describing manipulated flood control budgets and falsified completion reports. Her guests listened carefully. Some questioned her conclusions. Others remained silent. Wine glasses clinked, but the atmosphere grew heavier as the night went on.

---

## Phase 3 — The Night Before (Part 2)
As the gathering continued, smaller conversations formed. Two of the guests stepped aside, speaking in hushed tones about the consequences of exposure—political collapse, investigations, prison. Cassie remained in the background, holding the dossier, watching closely. Whatever trust once existed among them was visibly strained.

---

## Phase 4 — The Safe
Later that night, Cassie retreated to her study. She placed the dossier inside her wall-mounted safe, entering a six-digit numerical code she had carefully chosen. The safe clicked shut. The hard copy was secure—for now. Cassie believed that even if one layer failed, others would hold.

---

## Phase 5 — Morning Discovery
The following morning, Cassie’s driver, Jose Delos, entered the house to prepare for the day. In the main hall on the first floor, he found Cassie lying motionless on the floor, blood pooling beneath her. She was already dead. The guests were still inside the house. No one had left overnight.

---

## Phase 6 — Initial Assessment
The scene showed no signs of forced entry. Furniture was undisturbed. A blood trail suggested Cassie had moved from another part of the house before collapsing in the hall. The house layout made it clear: anyone inside that night could have reached her. This was not seen in the camera though. Could it be the glitched part of the camera?

---

## Phase 7 — The Empty Safe
When investigators opened the safe in Cassie’s study, it was empty. The hard-copy dossier was gone. There were faint smudge marks on the keypad—too many to identify a single person, but enough to confirm the safe had been opened after Cassie secured it.

---

## Phase 8 — The Camera Gap
Security footage from inside the house revealed a critical gap. Between 12:20 a.m. and 12:23 a.m., the cameras glitched. The blackout was brief—but long enough for someone to move unseen between rooms.

---

## Phase 9 — The Kitchen
In the kitchen, investigators found a crumpled napkin with handwritten digits: “2 – 7 – 6.” Nearby, a knife block sat on the counter, missing one large kitchen knife. The rest of the kitchen appeared clean, almost deliberately so.

---

## Phase 10 — Outside the House
Behind the house, muddy footprints led from a back door toward the garden before disappearing. The ground suggested someone had stepped outside briefly and returned, possibly to discard or hide something.

---

## Phase 11 — Personal Items (Part 1)
In one guest’s room, a legal pad lay on a bedside table. Several numbers were written repeatedly, some lightly circled. It was unclear whether they were calculations, notes, or something else entirely.

---

## Phase 11 — Personal Items (Part 2)
Under the same bed, investigators later discovered a napkin stained with blood, partially hidden from view. It matched the texture of the napkin found in the kitchen.

---

## Phase 12 — Burned Pages
In another guest’s trash bin, investigators found partially burned documents. Though damaged, fragments clearly showed flood control project titles, peso amounts, and ledger-style tables. It appeared to be part of Cassie’s dossier—but not all of it.

---

## Phase 13 — The Driver’s Words
Jose Delos gave his statement calmly. He spoke about Cassie’s habits, her belief in responsibility and compassion, and her fondness for stories where reason and conscience must coexist. He mentioned that Cassie always believed in preparing for the worst while hoping for the best.

Meanwhile, the investigator have the results on the fingerprints on safe.

---

## Phase 14 — Resolution
With the evidence laid out—the missing dossier, the burned fragments, the safe, the phone, and the shifting alibis—the case now rests in the detective’s hands. The truth is within reach, but only careful reasoning will reveal who killed Cassie Carval—and whether her work will still see the light of day.

---

## Phase 15 — The Second Phone
A second phone belonging to Cassie was discovered hidden in her study. When powered on, it displayed a single message: “Speak the trusted phrase.” No keypad appeared. Only those who truly understood Cassie’s safeguards would know what to enter.
